RESEARCH QUESTION
----------------------------------------
How do poverty and private-sector dominance physically compress
the effective healthcare service area for the urban poor,
independent of the total number of facilities in a city?

ANALYTICAL FRAMING (n=17)
----------------------------------------
Metro Manila has exactly 17 LGUs — the complete population, not
a sample.  The model is an ATTRIBUTION ANALYSIS: it decomposes
which structural factors most explain variation in geographic
isolation across NCR cities.  R² is interpreted as 'the features
collectively account for X% of variance in healthcare access' —
a descriptive structural claim, not a generalisation to unseen data.

REGRESSION TARGETS
----------------------------------------
Task A — Wd (2SFCA-inspired Weighted Accessibility Index)
  Combines public/private L3 supply weighted by poverty discount
  and Haversine distance. Higher = more accessible.

Task B — Effective Public Beds per 1,000 residents
  = total_beds × (1 − private_ownership_pct) / population_per_1000
  Government-only inpatient depth — what the poor can actually access.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
----------------------------------------
1. n=17: Complete population census — LOO R² is descriptive,
   not a generalisation estimate.
2. City centroids: Haversine underestimates within-city variation
   (Quezon City is 165 km² — peripheral barangays may be 15+ km
   by road despite the 1.57 km centroid distance).
3. Temporal mismatch: NHFR ~2023–2025, population 2024,
   poverty 2023. Cross-sectional snapshot, not longitudinal.
4. Private/public binary: Ignores PhilHealth coverage,
   NGO hospitals, and user fees in public hospitals.
5. NCR-only scope: Poverty range 0.3–4.2% (national avg ~18%).
   Model would not transfer to other regions.
